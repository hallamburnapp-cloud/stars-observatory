Supervisory Machinery — National Space Legislation

Largest payload populations under a State with no dedicated space law

Article VI OST. States Parties bear international responsibility for national activities in outer space, "whether such activities are carried on by governmental agencies or by non-governmental entities," and must ensure they are conducted with authorisation and continuing supervision. The chart quantifies the supervisory burden: a doctrine written for a handful of state programmes now attaches to tens of thousands of privately operated, software-defined objects concentrated under a few licensing States.

Caveat: Registration submissions to UNOOSA lag launch by months to years. Most 2025–26 launches are therefore pending rather than definitively unregistered. But the lag itself is the finding: the State-based registry structurally trails operational reality by the very interval in which harmful interference must be prevented.

Registration Lag Index — Live Longitudinal Dataset

Registration Convention (1975), Art II. A launching State "shall register" the space object by entry in an appropriate registry and inform the UN Secretary-General. The registry is the foundational transparency instrument the OST architecture presupposes — identifying who bears responsibility and who must be consulted. Where it demonstrably trails the catalog, the doctrinal machinery of Articles VI and IX loses its factual anchor.

Limitations

  • TLE / SGP4 positions carry km-scale error and degrade with propagation age; this is not an operational conjunction-assessment (SSA) system.
  • Classified and untracked objects are excluded from the public catalog entirely.
  • Sub-10 cm debris is largely untrackable — an estimated 1,000,000+ fragments are not represented here.
  • UN registration submissions lag launch by months to years; recent "unregistered" payloads may be pending rather than in breach.
  • Ownership / responsible-State attribution follows the 18 SDS catalog convention and may differ from the formal "launching State" under the Registration Convention.
  • 15,563 cataloged objects (largely debris fragments and rocket bodies without public GP element sets) are counted in the statistics but not propagated in the 3D view.
This instrument fuses publicly available tracking and registration data with a legal-responsibility overlay. It is a demonstrative artifact supporting doctrinal analysis of State preventive obligations under Articles VI and IX of the Outer Space Treaty — not an operational SSA system. Built and maintained by Hallam Burnapp, PhD researcher in international space law, University of Aberdeen.
