# National space law review — 24 September 2026

Every entry in `site/data/national_law.json` (106 SATCAT owner codes) was researched afresh against the UNOOSA National Space Law database, official legislation portals and gazettes, regulators and parliamentary records, as a full legal framework rather than a single headline instrument: primary statutes and amendments, implementing regulations, licensing and registry rules, liability and insurance rules, debris and safety conditions, remote-sensing and spectrum licensing where that is how a State authorises satellites, recent enactments (2023–2026) and pending bills. Each instrument is judged for relevance to the functions STARS measures (Article VI authorisation and continuing supervision; Article VIII registration; Article VII liability; Article IX due regard) and tagged core / supporting / context. Items a reader might expect but that were judged not relevant are listed with reasons.

**Classification rule.** yes = an enacted, in-force instrument with binding legal force (statute, or binding regulation/decree) establishes an authorisation regime for non-governmental space activities (remote-sensing-only regimes count and are flagged); no = no such regime (agency-creation, registration-only, telecoms/spectrum-only, State monopoly without private authorisation, or drafts only); consortium = intergovernmental organisation, company or multi-State programme code; unknown = not determinable.

- 36 yes · 45 no · 24 consortium · 1 unknown (TBD, unattributed objects).
- 469 instruments recorded; 77 considered and judged not relevant.
- Classification changes since the 24 September morning review: **ALG** yes → no; **RWA** yes → no; **AZER** yes → no; **NKOR** yes → no.

## Borderline classifications (author to confirm)

- **PRC** (China, `yes`): No statute: the licensing and registration regime rests on departmental rules (bumen guizhang) of COSTIND (now SASTIND/CNSA). These are binding secondary legislation, so 'yes' holds, but the national Space Law is still only in draft.
- **IND** (India, `no`): IN-SPACe authorises non-governmental space activity in practice under the Indian Space Policy 2023 and its 2024 Norms, Guidelines and Procedures. These are executive instruments with no statutory basis or force of law, so the result is 'no' under the strict rule. The Space Activities Bill is still unintroduced.
- **ITSO** (Intelsat (ITSO), `consortium`): ITSO is an IGO that no longer operates satellites; the privatised Intelsat fleet (owned by SES since July 2025) is licensed mainly by the US FCC, with the US as registering State for those objects.
- **IT** (Italy, `yes`): Law 89/2025 is in force (25 June 2025) and makes space activities subject to authorisation, but the Art. 13 implementing DPCMs (requirements, file, fees, insurance bands) were not found adopted as of Sept 2026.
- **GER** (Germany, `yes`): Remote-sensing-only: SatDSiG licenses operators of high-grade Earth remote-sensing systems; no general authorisation regime for other space activities. National Weltraumgesetz still an unpublished ministry draft.
- **GLOB** (Globalstar (US-licensed commercial MSS operator), `consortium`): Code denotes a US commercial operator, not a multi-State body; kept as 'consortium' per dataset convention (Globalstar began as a Loral/Qualcomm partnership). Supervising State is the US.
- **CA** (Canada, `yes`): Remote-sensing-only: the RSSSA is the only in-force private authorisation regime. Other satellites are authorised only via ISED spectrum licences; launches only case-by-case under the Aeronautics Act/CARs until Bill C-28 passes. No statutory national registry.
- **SES** (SES (Luxembourg), `consortium`): Commercial operator, not a State: Luxembourg authorises/registers Luxembourg-licensed satellites; US-licensed fleets (SES Americom, ex-Intelsat since July 2025) are licensed by the FCC.
- **EUTE** (Eutelsat, `consortium`): Commercial company (ex-IGO), not a State: GEO fleet authorised and registered by France; OneWeb LEO fleet licensed and registered by the UK.
- **ORB** (ORBCOMM (US-licensed commercial operator), `consortium`): Code denotes a US commercial operator (privately held since 2021), not a consortium; retained as 'consortium' per dataset convention. Supervising State is the US.
- **ARGN** (Argentina, `no`): CONAE has sole competence over the national space programme under Decree 995/91, and a registry exists (Decree 125/95). No statute or decree creates a licensing regime for non-governmental operators. A comprehensive bill is pending (March 2026).
- **O3B** (O3b / O3b mPOWER (SES group; UK-licensed and UK-registered), `consortium`): Commercial operator in a multinational group (SES, Luxembourg), not a multi-State body; retained as 'consortium'. Licensing and registration State is the UK (via OSA and its Jersey extension).
- **BRAZ** (Brazil, `yes`): Law 14,946/2024 requires the AEB and Air Force to update their regulations within 365 days (Art. 47). No post-2024 AEB regulation was found, so licensing still runs under REB Parts 1–3 (2021–2022).
- **IM** (Inmarsat (ex-IGO, privatised 1999; Viasat subsidiary since 2023), `consortium`): Former intergovernmental organisation now a private UK company; retained as 'consortium'. Operative supervising State is the UK.
- **AB** (Arab Satellite Communications Organization (Arabsat), `consortium`): No national authorisation instrument verified for Arabsat satellites; responsibility rests with member States collectively (OST Art. VI/XIII). Saudi (host State) space instruments could not be verified (CST site JS-only).
- **SAUD** (Saudi Arabia, `no`): CST Ordinance as amended by CoM Res. 430 (2024/25) lets CST license the space sector, and CST registers telecom space stations, but no space-activities licensing law or regulation is in force; CST rules still draft
- **PAKI** (Pakistan, `yes`): The Rules are binding secondary legislation (S.R.O. in the Gazette Extraordinary, in force immediately) made by the NCA under the National Command Authority Act 2010, not a dedicated space statute. They are the only binding authorisation basis, and Pakistan's 2025 COPUOS submission lists only its space policy.
- **IRAN** (Iran, `no`): The Statute of the Iranian Space Agency (Cabinet, 2005, under Art. 9 of the 2003 ICT Ministry Law) lists 'issuing the authorization for space activities' among ISA's tasks. But no published authorisation regime (procedure, conditions) was found, and the statute is an agency-creation instrument. Hence 'no'.
- **MEX** (Mexico, `no`): The 2025 telecom law gives the CRT concessions over orbital resources and makes deorbiting a GSO satellite subject to CRT authorisation (Art. 140). Under the rule this is telecom/spectrum licensing, not a space-activity authorisation regime.
- **NATO** (North Atlantic Treaty Organization (NATO satellites II, III, IV), `consortium`): US instruments for the earlier NATO II/III/IVA satellites (US launches, US registration) not separately listed: military government payloads, no licensing statute applied.
- **AC** (AsiaSat (Asia Satellite Telecommunications Co. Ltd, Hong Kong SAR), `consortium`): Commercial operator in a sub-national jurisdiction (HKSAR), not a consortium; retained. Post-1997 UN registration via China not individually verified.
- **ABS** (ABS / Agility Beyond Space (commercial operator; Bermuda, redomiciled to Dubai 2025), `consortium`): Commercial operator, not a consortium. Supervising State in transition: Bermuda/UK until Oct 2025; UAE thereafter only if ABS obtained a UAE Space Agency permit (not confirmed). ABS satellites are not on the UK registers (Aug 2026).
- **ALG** (Algeria, `no`): Law 19-06 is a full space-activities statute (registry at ASAL, liability, risk plans) but Art. 5 makes space activities an exclusive State monopoly with no private authorisation; 'no' under the monopoly rule
- **SWTZ** (Switzerland, `no`): Federal Act on Space Operations (bill 26.032) passed the National Council on 24 Sept 2026 (191-0, 2 abstentions); Council of States yet to vote. The classification changes only on enactment.
- **RWA** (Rwanda, `no`): Law 022/2021 Art. 8(2) lets RSA issue permits 'in accordance with relevant governing laws and regulations', but no space law or licensing regulation exists yet; agency-creation law only
- **CHBZ** (China/Brazil joint programme (CBERS), `consortium`): Objects: CBERS 1 (1999-057A, plus ~90 fragmentation debris), CBERS 2 (2003-049A), 2B (2007-042A), 4 (2014-079A), 4A (2019-093E). Government programme; registering State varies by satellite. China measures' text not fetched.
- **NIG** (Nigeria, `yes`): Licensing powers existed since 2010 and the 2015 Regulations were only gazetted in Sept 2021 (S.I. 110); NASRDA began operational licensing in 2025
- **AZER** (Azerbaijan, `no`): Law No. 927-VIQ (2023) sets up state registration of title to space objects, Ministry supervision, prior notification of foreign acquisitions, certification and third-party insurance. It contains no licence or permit to carry out space activity. Private actors act 'by means of a space operator', and only orbital and spectrum use is licensed (Licences and Permits and Telecoms laws). Hence 'no' under the strict rule.
- **VENZ** (Venezuela, `no`): The ABAE Law gives ABAE power to propose and issue norms on space matters. No authorisation or licensing regime for non-governmental space activities has been adopted, and space activities are State-run.
- **FGER** (France/Germany joint (Symphonie 1 and 2), `consortium`): Historic government programme; no authorising instrument applied. Status of German Space Act after Feb 2026 not verifiable.
- **STCT** (Singapore/Taiwan joint (SingTel–Chunghwa Telecom: ST-1, ST-2), `consortium`): Neither State authorises operation of these satellites under a space statute; Singapore has only signed (not ratified) REG (CRP.9). Both launched by Ariane from Kourou.
- **RASC** (RASCOM / RascomStar-QAF (Regional African Satellite Communication Organization), `consortium`): Objects: RASCOM-QAF 1 (2007-063A) and 1R (2010-037B). No State authorisation or UN registration found; Mauritius (company seat) is not party to LIAB or REG (CRP.9, 1 Jan 2025).
- **FRIT** (France/Italy joint (Athena-Fidus, SICRAL 2), `consortium`): Government dual-use programme (CNES/ASI/defence ministries): each object is registered by one State. Defence carve-outs in LOS (2022 amendments) and Law 89/2025 may exclude these objects; not confirmed.
- **ISS** (International Space Station (multi-State programme), `consortium`): SATCAT ISS payload records: ISS (ZARYA) 1998-067A, ISS (UNITY) 1998-069F, ISS (ZVEZDA) 2000-037A, ISS (DESTINY) 2001-006B, plus ISS-shed debris. Russian and Japanese implementing laws not separately verified.
- **SEAL** (Sea Launch (multinational launch consortium), `consortium`): Objects: SEA LAUNCH DEMO (1999-014A) and c. two dozen BLOCK DM-SL R/B stages (e.g. 1999-014B) catalogued to SEAL. UN registration of these stages not found; US as launching (licensing) State.
- **NICO** (New ICO / ICO Global Communications (ICO F2, now Omnispace OMNI-M1), `consortium`): Commercial operator code (ex-ICO, now Omnispace, US) with one object; retained as 'consortium'. Supervising and registering State: US.
- **USBZ** (United States/Brazil joint (Estrela do Sul 1), `consortium`): Commercial satellite built by US Loral for a Brazilian subsidiary; code reflects US/Brazil involvement. Brazil is State of registry; US role limited to launch licensing.
- **TMMC** (Turkmenistan/Monaco joint (TürkmenÄlem 52°E / MonacoSAT), `consortium`): Neither State was party to OST/LIAB/REG as at 1 Jan 2025 (Monaco: OST signature only); satellite not UN-registered. Turkmen law number (input: 307-V) not verified.
- **LAOS** (Laos, `no`): Decree 471/GOL (2019) requires Lao legal entities that establish or buy satellites to register with the telecom ministry, which then files with the ITU (Art 21). This is a satellite-communications and spectrum registration, not an authorisation of space activities.
- **GRSA** (Greece/Saudi Arabia joint (Hellas Sat 4 / SaudiGeoSat-1), `consortium`): Greece is licensing/registering State. Saudi Arabia (party to OST/LIAB/REG) co-owns via KACST; Saudi space instruments not verified. Law 4508 cited via lawspot (official FEK site unreachable).
- **NKOR** (North Korea, `no`): The Law on Space Development (2013, revised Aug 2022) regulates design, launch and operation of satellites through NADA. However, all space actors are State organs; there is no authorisation of non-governmental entities (State monopoly). No official full text is available online, only KCNA reporting. Hence 'no' under the strict rule.
